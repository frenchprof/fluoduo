Les repas et les aliments

What I eat and drink at each meal

📚Tier 2 · Lexical Core

0🗺 Learning path

The same five stages as a grammar lesson, in the same order — learning path · concept · forms · exercise · bonus · word list. What changes is what goes inside two of them.

  1. 1💡 The concept — one question the word list cannot answer.
  2. 2📖 The forms — what each word makes you learn along with it.
  3. 3📝 The exercise — sort the words, with less and less help.
  4. 4⭐ The bonus — English to French, no hints. Aim for 80%.
  5. 5📚 The word list — all 28 words, with test modes.
What changed Nothing structural. My first draft invented a new stage called the map; that was wrong. A vocabulary lesson runs the same five stages — only the contents of stages 2 and 3 differ, because a noun's forms are not a verb's forms and sorting words is not conjugating them.

1💡 The concept

Do not skip this.

One question

A French meal has a course that an English meal has no slot for. Which one — and when does it arrive?

The answer, in one sentence

Cheese — le fromage is its own course, after the dish and before the dessert. So in French du fromage is not a topping or a snack: it has a place in the order of a meal, and the word carries that place with it.

And a second thing.

Five of these words are ingredients — du sucre, de la farine, de l'huile, du sel, des oignons. You don't order them, you cook with them, and that makes them their own category rather than an odd leftover: a learner who can follow a recipe needs them exactly as much as one who can read a menu.

Why this is still a concept A Tier 1 concept is a question the forms cannot answer — why son cahier is both his and hers. A Tier 2 concept is a question the word list cannot answer. Same move, different target: no vocabulary table will ever tell you where cheese comes.

2📖 The forms

A noun has forms too. Not a conjugation — a gender, which you cannot work out from the meaning and have to learn with the word, the way you learn its spelling.

The third column is the lesson. Nine of these 42 words are stored in a form that hides the gender — de l' before a vowel, des in the plural. A learner who only ever meets de l'eau has never been told that eau is feminine. And nothing in aliments.json records gender as a field, so the three columns above are inferred from the article in the string. That is a content fix before it is a design one.

And the other thing a food word makes you learn: à or de?

Compound food names come in two shapes, and English does not distinguish them — "apple tart" and "apple juice" are built the same way. French builds them differently, and the difference is how much of the thing is that ingredient.

à + article + noun

One important ingredient, among others.

  • une tarte aux pommes apple tart
  • une glace à la vanille vanilla ice cream
  • un sandwich au fromage cheese sandwich
  • un café au lait coffee with milk

de + noun, no article

What the thing is made of — the main ingredient, often the only one.

  • un jus de pomme apple juice
  • du jus d'orange orange juice — in this deck
  • une soupe de poisson fish soup
  • une salade de fruits fruit salad

Same fruit, two shapes: une tarte aux pommes — apples in it, several of them, so the noun goes plural — against un jus de pomme, where the apple is what the juice is, so it stays singular. The preposition carries the number with it.

Only one of the 42 shows this. Du jus d'orange is the deck's single compound name, so the pattern above is drawn from outside it. Same question as the exercise frame: either the deck grows a handful of compounds, or a Tier 2 lesson is allowed to teach with words it does not own.

Where this came from Your suggestion — a vocab list with gender and so on, as in SpecuLearn. It is a better answer than the one I drew, because it is genuinely a forms stage: something un-derivable that must be learned alongside the word. My version showed what the words mean, which is not the same job.
The colours are borrowed, not invented Blue and red are --gram-masc and --gram-fem, the tokens colour review mapped across all 98 sites. Here they mean exactly what they were defined to mean, so they are the right tokens rather than a new pair.

3📝 The exercise

One sentence, three levels — the same mechanism as the grammar ladder. What is withdrawn each time is a piece of the answer, and the first piece to go is the article, because that is the part the forms tab says you cannot work out.

The frame carries two article slots, because avec takes the partitive all over again — and the second one is the one learners drop.

★

Choose it whole

Article and noun arrive together. Pick the one that fits.

🥖 « Je prends du pain avec __________. »
du beurre · de la confiture · des frites
★★

Fill in the article

Both nouns are given. Supply both articles — so the gender has to be known, not recognised, twice.

« Je prends ____ pain avec ____ beurre. » du … du
★★★

Fill in both

Article and noun, all four slots, from the cue alone.

🥖🧈 « Je prends ____ ______ avec ____ ______. » du pain avec du beurre

One word here is not in this deck. De la confiture lives in partitifs.json, the stop 42 deck, not in aliments.json — and it is the natural partner for du pain. So the frame either draws from both decks or aliments gains the word. Worth settling early: if a Tier 2 exercise may only use its own deck, that constrains every frame in all fifteen stops.

This changes Track A's priority I had written that Tier 2 needed no type change. With ★★ blanking the article and ★★★ blanking article and noun, that is no longer true: this is a two-slot cloze, the same shape as ★★ in L08. DiceQuestion.med holds one blank fixed by the generator, so neither tier can express its own ladder today. A1 now unblocks 35 stops rather than 20, which makes it the most load-bearing thing left in the container.

4⭐ The bonus  ·  5📚 The word list

Both unchanged from a grammar lesson, and deliberately so. The bonus is English → French with no hints, aiming at 80%. The word list is the reference table for all 42 words, both directions, click a cell to reveal.

Why leave them alone A tier is a difference in treatment, not a different app. Two of the five stages already do the right thing for every tier; changing them would be change for its own sake.

Three things to rule on

  1. A concept stage for a vocabulary stop — is the cheese course the right kind of question, or should a Lexical Core lesson simply not have a concept stage?
  2. Gender as "the forms" — your answer, drawn here. Is the third column (the seven words whose article hides their gender) the right thing to build a stage around?
  3. The frame — « Je prends du pain avec du beurre » gives two article decisions in one sentence, which is what makes the cloze worth doing. Does one frame carry all 28 words, or does a vocabulary stop need a small set of frames and a rule for which word takes which?
