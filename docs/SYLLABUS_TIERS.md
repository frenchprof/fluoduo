# The three-tiered syllabus — all 50 stops classified

**Draft by the Peers session, 30 Aug 2026, from Dan's architecture. For Dan to
correct.** Nothing in the code reads this file yet; it is the map that decides
which treatment each stop gets, and therefore what Track C authors write.

## The architecture, as given

| tier | what it contains | treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Systemic Grammar** (The Rules) | closed-class systems, inflectional paradigms, generative rules | accuracy-focused; rule-discovery; structural drills |
| **2 · Lexical Core** (The Units) | open-class items, semantic fields, core word meanings | meaning-focused; semantic mapping; contextual retrieval |
| **3 · Phraseology & Lexicogrammar** (The Bridge) | collocations, semi-fixed structures, high-frequency chunks, pragmatic formulas | pattern-recognition and chunking; learned as holistic blocks |

## The finding that matters

**Dan's three approved pages are all Tier 1.** `08 aimer le/la/les`,
`09 faire du/de la`, `13 possessifs` — every one a closed-class system with a
generative rule. The pathway he approved (Le concept → Les formes → the
★/★★/★★★ scaffold ladder) is *the Tier 1 treatment*, not a universal one.

That is very likely the origin of the drift he named. Forty-odd stops needed a
shape; only the Tier 1 shape was written down; so the other two tiers grew
whatever was to hand — EtuDice, Sorting, Letris. The mistake was not that
Tiers 2 and 3 got activities. It was that they got activities *instead of* a
designed treatment.

**Twenty stops (40%) are Tier 1.** Fixing the ladder and writing concepts serves
those twenty completely and the rest not at all.

## Tier 3 · Phraseology — 9 stops

Whole blocks. Not assembled from parts, and analysing them into parts is
actively wrong at this level: *il fait beau* is not IL + FAIRE + BEAU to a
beginner, it is the weather. Treatment is recognition and retrieval of the
block, plus knowing **when** to say it.

## Ateliers — 6 stops, no tier

10, 20, 30, 40, 49, 50. Production. These are where all three tiers are spent

## What this changes in the plan

`docs/LESSON_SPEC_PLAN.md` assumes one pathway for every stop and asks, in its
"Decide before starting" §1, whether that is right. **This answers it: no.**
Three tiers, three treatments.

1. **Track A and Track C, as written, serve Tier 1 — 20 stops.** That work is
   correct and unchanged. It is not 44 lessons of authoring; it is 20.
2. **Tier 2's treatment is undesigned.** Track D (Le lexique) is the beginning
   of it and should be promoted out of "low priority" — for 15 stops it is not
   a side surface, it *is* the lesson.
3. **Tier 3's treatment is undesigned.** Chunk-level: recognise the block,
   choose the right block for the situation. Not a gap-fill.
4. **The activity cull becomes answerable.** An activity is not good or bad in
   the abstract — it is right or wrong *for a tier*. Sorting is plainly a Tier 2
   instrument (semantic mapping). The question to put to Dan is per activity:
   which tier, or none.

## Uncertain — for Dan

- **2 Tu/Vous** — filed Tier 1 as a closed system, but the whole difficulty is
  pragmatic, which is Tier 3's territory.
- **34 Questions** — the interrogative system is Tier 1, but *est-ce que* is
  learned as a block long before it is analysed.
- **43 Frequency** — placement is a rule (1); the adverb set is small and
  semi-closed (2).
- **3 Alphabet** — an inventory, but not open-class and not phraseology. It may
  simply sit outside the three tiers.
